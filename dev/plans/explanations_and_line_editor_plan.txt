Two plans (2026-11-08): the missing "How to read this" boxes, and an editor for dashes and markers
=================================================================================================

5. "How to read this" for every analysis
----------------------------------------
21 analyses explain their key numbers to a student (how_to_read, Settings > Reports > Explain); 17 do not:
  Baseline Correction, Calculus, Clustering, Distribution, Filtering, Function, Gaussian Process, Geometry,
  Has it changed over time?, Interpolation, Is it in control?, Is it normal?, Peaks, Roots, Smoothing, Transform,
  Wafer Map.
First a check: "Is it in control?" and "Has it changed over time?" may inherit Control Chart's and Time Series'
boxes through their base; those that do are left out.

A. Each gets how_to_read(results): two to four entries, each a number the report hinges on and two sentences on
   what it says about these data, in the words of the existing ones (app.plugin_api.reading_guide). Written from
   the result's own values, never generic. For example:
   - Is it normal?: the Shapiro-Wilk / Anderson-Darling p-value (below 0.05: the data are unlikely to be normal;
     with thousands of rows even a harmless departure shows), skewness and kurtosis (which way it leans, how heavy
     its tails), and the normal probability plot (points on the line: normal).
   - Clustering: the silhouette (near 1 well apart, near 0 overlapping), the number of clusters chosen and why,
     each cluster's size (one tiny cluster: perhaps outliers).
   - Peaks: how many were found against the prominence asked, the main peak's position and width (FWHM), and the
     signal-to-noise behind them.
   - Smoothing / Filtering: the residual's spread against the data's (how much was taken out), and the window or
     cut-off in the data's own units.
   - Roots / Calculus / Interpolation / Function: what the number is (a root's x, an area under the curve with its
     units, an interpolated value) and how far to trust it (the bracket, the step, extrapolation outside the data).
   - Distribution: the fitted distribution and its parameters, the goodness of fit (AIC compared with the next
     best), and the capability indices when limits are set.
   - Gaussian Process: the length scale (how far a point's influence reaches), the noise level, and the
     prediction band (wide where there are no data).
   - Wafer Map: the mean and spread across the wafer, the radial or edge pattern if one shows, the yield.
   Strings listed for translation, not translated.
B. A test that every table analysis and data operation gives at least one entry for a typical result (the tests'
   showcase data), so a new analysis without a box is noticed.
Order: the statistics ones first (normal, distribution, clustering, in control, changed), then the signal ones
(peaks, smoothing, filtering, baseline, spectral already has one), then the numeric ones (roots, calculus,
interpolation, function, transform, geometry), then GP and wafer map. One commit per group.


6. An editor for line dashes and markers
----------------------------------------
Today a series' line style is one of matplotlib's four ("-", "--", "-.", ":") and its marker one of its letters.

A. Dashes - a "Line style" field in Series properties with a "..." button opening the editor:
   - the named ones, and matplotlib's own patterns typed as they are known: "-", "--", "-.", ":", and the
     dash tuple (offset, (on, off, on, off, ...)) in points;
   - a pattern typed as text: "-- . -- ." or "..---" (a dash per "-", a dot per ".", a space per " "), turned
     into the tuple, scaled by the line width as matplotlib does;
   - a live preview of the line at the series' width and colour; cap style (butt, round, projecting) and join;
   - saved in the series' style as the tuple, so matplotlib draws it and every export keeps it.
B. Markers - the same "..." on the Marker field:
   - matplotlib's markers in a grid to pick from ("o", "s", "^", "D", "*", "P", "X", "h", "$text$"...);
   - a regular polygon or star: sides, rotation, inner radius ((n, 0|1|2, angle));
   - a marker drawn from text or a symbol: "$♥$", any character or short TeX;
   - a path: a small editor of points (click to add, drag to move) or an SVG path ("M0,0 L1,1 ..."), turned into
     a matplotlib Path, normalised to the marker size; edge and fill colour and edge width;
   - user markers kept by name in the project (a table __markers__), offered beside matplotlib's.
   - pictures as markers are the other plan (image_markers_plan.txt) and share its picker.
C. Tests: text patterns to tuples ("..---" and friends), the tuple drawn the same in PNG and SVG, an SVG path to a
   Path, a stored marker reloaded with the project.
Risks: some renderers draw their own lines (box plots, fills) and ignore a dash tuple - the editor is offered only
where the series is drawn with plot()/scatter() and says so elsewhere.
Order: A (dashes), B (markers), C alongside.
