Pictures as markers and bars: infographics drawn from data (2026-11-08)
=======================================================================

What it is
----------
A scatter's points drawn as pictures (a flag per country, an icon per species), and a bar drawn as a picture -
stretched, or stacked as a pictogram (one person = 1,000 people, the last one cut to the fraction). The pictures
are kept in the project, so it travels whole and exports the same everywhere.

A. Storage: a table __images__ (name TEXT PRIMARY KEY, kind 'svg'|'png'|'jpeg', data BLOB, source TEXT, license
   TEXT, added TEXT). SVG kept as text (sharp at any size, recoloured by the series' colour when it is a
   single-colour icon); PNG/JPEG kept as they are, at most 1024 px a side (larger ones scaled on import).
   Undo covers adding and removing one. The Tables list does not show it (a "__" table), Project > Images does.

B. Where pictures come from - an "Images" dialog (Project page, and from each picture field):
   - Import from a file (SVG, PNG, JPEG), or paste from the clipboard;
   - A small library shipped with the app, from open sets whose licence lets a chart that uses them be shared:
     Lucide (ISC) or Tabler Icons (MIT) - about 200 icons chosen for charts (people, money, buildings, transport,
     weather, animals, flags of the world from flag-icons (MIT)); searchable by name;
   - Online, on request: Iconify's search (iconify.design, 200,000 icons from open sets), each with its set's
     licence shown and stored with it; nothing is sent before the user asks, as for the web sources.
   - The system's own symbols are not offered for charts: Apple's SF Symbols licence allows them in the user
     interface of Apple-platform apps only, not in images exported or shared; Windows' Segoe Fluent glyphs and the
     Linux icon themes have licences of their own. The app keeps using them for its buttons, as it does today.

C. In the charts (one renderer option each, in Series properties):
   - Scatter / bubble: "Marker picture" - one picture, or a column whose values name pictures (species -> icon),
     sized by the marker size (or the bubble's size); matplotlib OffsetImage/AnnotationBbox, SVG rasterised at the
     figure's dpi through QSvgRenderer (no new package).
   - Bar / column: "Bar picture" - stretched to the bar, or repeated: a pictogram with "one picture = N" (N chosen,
     or nice-rounded from the data), the last picture clipped to the fraction; a legend line says what one stands for.
   - Pie / donut: a picture in each slice, or in the hole.
   - Text and title: a picture beside the title (a logo).
   Colours: a single-colour SVG takes the series' colour; a multicolour one or a photo keeps its own.

D. Export: SVG and PDF keep the pictures as vectors where they were SVG; PNG at the export's dpi.

E. Tests: the table (add, replace, undo), SVG recolouring, a pictogram's count and clipped last picture, a scatter
   with a picture column exported to SVG and PNG, offscreen.

Risks
- Many pictures (a scatter of 5,000 flags) draw slowly: above a threshold the renderer says so and draws a sample,
  as the downsampling does for points.
- Licences: every picture keeps its source and licence; the export can list them (credits) in the report.

Strings listed for translation, not translated. Order: A, C for the scatter (usable), B, C for bars, D, E alongside.


Other ideas for infographics (not planned yet - to choose from)
---------------------------------------------------------------
1. Big number cards: a "KPI" chart type - a value in large type, its unit, the change from the previous period
   (arrow, colour) and a sparkline; several in a row as a dashboard strip.
2. Waffle chart: a 10x10 grid of squares (or pictures) filled to a share - "37 of 100".
3. Annotations with callouts: a label with a line or an arrow to a point, a highlighted band, a note box -
   placed by dragging, kept with the chart (beyond today's text and reference lines).
4. Themes for infographics: bolder fonts, flat colours, no axes frame, large titles and a subtitle and a source
   line at the bottom ("Source: ..."), as newspapers draw charts.
5. Layout of several charts on one page with text blocks between them (a poster or a report page), exported as
   one PDF or PNG.
6. Maps: a choropleth of countries or regions coloured by a value (shapes shipped as GeoJSON, no new package).
7. Progress and gauge visuals: a progress bar, a ring, a half-donut gauge with its target.
8. Highlighting: grey everything but the series or the bars that matter, with one click (the "focus" of a story).
9. Slope and bump charts already exist; a "before/after" arrow chart could join them.
10. Number formatting for infographics: 1.2k, 3.4M, percentages, units in labels, everywhere at once.
